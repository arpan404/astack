# Interface copy

Words in an interface help people act. Every string earns its place.

## Domain language

- Use the words people in the domain already use. A clinic has patients and appointments, not records, entities, or items.
- Name things by what the user controls and recognizes, never by how the system is built: "Notifications", not "Webhook settings". Implementation stays out of sight: IDs, status codes, field names, enum values, "sync", "payload", "instance", "null".
- Write for the real audience's reading level, and in their language. A site crew needs simpler words than a developer, and may need more than one language.
- One name per thing and per action across the product. The "Publish" button leads to "Published".

## No commentary

- No helper paragraphs under headings, no "Here you can...", no "This section shows...", no captions restating what's visible, no tooltips on self-explanatory controls, no marketing language inside the product.
- When a screen seems to need explaining, fix the design. Add words only where a person would otherwise be stuck, and keep them short.

## Writing

- Buttons are verbs that name the outcome: "Save changes", "Send invoice". Not "Submit", "OK", or "Yes".
- Sentence case, plain words, active voice, no exclamation marks.
- Errors say what happened and how to fix it, in the interface's voice, without apologizing or blaming.
- Empty states invite the action that fills them.
- Numbers, dates, and units in the user's locale; relative time where recency matters.
- None of the AI voice: seamless, effortless, powerful, unlock, supercharge, elevate, delightful, magic, journey.
- Realistic content in every design and prototype, never lorem ipsum or "Jane Doe".
